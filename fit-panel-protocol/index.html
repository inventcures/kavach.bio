1. Title

Head-and-face anthropometry and respirator fit among Indian health workers: a multi-region study to build an Indian respirator fit panel, headforms and a sized mask portfolio for pandemic stockpiles.

2. Background (one paragraph)

Respirators sold and stockpiled in India are designed and certified against US-derived faces. The NIOSH 2003 survey had no analysable Asian group. The ISO 16976-2 headforms (adopted in India as IS 17274-5) come from that survey. India’s own standard, IS 9473:2002, tests leakage on 10 unspecified clean-shaven subjects with no face-size grid.

India has no published head-and-face survey using respirator landmarks and no published fit-test pass rates for Indian wearers. Indian hospitals largely did not fit-test during COVID-19. The best proxy is UK data: South Asian health workers have lower FFP3 pass rates (Asian women 60.3% vs White men 74.3%). Every Asian country that has checked found its faces distributed differently from the NIOSH panel (China, Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia).

This study fills that gap and produces three things: an Indian fit panel, Indian headforms, and evidence on which existing masks (disposable and elastomeric) fit Indian health workers. These are the inputs a national respirator stockpile and a BIS standard amendment need.

3. Aims and objectives

Primary aim. Describe the head-and-face size and shape distribution of Indian health workers across six regions, and build an ISO-compatible Indian respirator fit panel.

Objectives

# Objective Output
O1 Measure the ISO/NIOSH 10 core and ~19 total facial dimensions, plus nose-bridge and chin measures, by calliper and 3D scan Indian anthropometric database (n ≈ 3,000)
O2 Quantify what share of Indian health workers fall inside each cell of the NIOSH/ISO bivariate and PCA panels, overall and by sex, age and region “Coverage gap” estimates: the first published figures for India
O3 Build Indian bivariate (10-cell, 25-subject) and PCA (8-cell) panels with ≥95% coverage Indian fit panel spec, ready to propose to BIS
O4 Build 3–5 Indian digital headforms from the scans using Procrustes shape analysis + PCA Open headform files (STL)
O5 Measure quantitative fit of 5–6 N95 models and 2–3 elastomeric half masks commonly procured in India; relate fit to dimensions, cells and leak sites Pass rates by model × sex × region × cell; the evidence for which portfolio to stockpile
O6 Compare the user seal check against the quantitative fit test Sensitivity and specificity of current Indian practice
O7 Validate smartphone (TrueDepth) scans against callipers and a reference scanner Bias per dimension, deciding if phones can scale fit-sizing nationally
O8 Co-design sized face seals and/or a size range with an Indian manufacturer, and benchmark prototypes on the new panel Prototype report (Phase 3)

Hypotheses (to test, not to assume): - H1. The NIOSH bivariate panel covers fewer than 95% of Indian women health workers. - H2. Indian faces are unevenly spread across NIOSH cells, with over-representation of the small and short/wide cells relative to US workers. - H3. Nose-bridge and nasal-root dimensions predict fit failure better than face length or width. - H4. Fit differs more between mask models than between regions. - H5. Sized elastomeric half masks achieve higher first-attempt pass rates than the most-procured disposable N95s.

5. Setting and sampling

5.2 Population

Who is eligible. Health workers aged 18–65: doctors, nurses, technicians, housekeeping and sanitation staff, ASHAs and ANMs attached to the site. These are the people a pandemic stockpile is meant to protect. Including lower-paid cadres avoids the medical-student bias of existing Indian data.

Who is excluded: - People who cannot wear a tight-fitting respirator for medical reasons (screen with the OSHA questionnaire adapted for India). - Facial surgery or trauma in the past year. - Pregnancy beyond 28 weeks, as a fit-test comfort exclusion only. Measurement can still go ahead.

Facial hair is not an exclusion. Record it, test bearded participants with and without the Singh Thattha under-mask beard cover, and analyse them separately.

5.3 Quotas

Quotas apply within each zone (n = 500 per zone):

18–29 30–44 45–65
Women 85 85 80
Men 85 85 80

Women are deliberately sampled at 50%, above their share of some cadres, because they fail fit tests most. Analysis weights to each zone’s health-workforce sex × age structure (from NHWA/PLFS or state HR data [TBC]) restore national estimates.

Recruitment works by open invitation through notices and staff WhatsApp groups. It does not go through supervisors (see ICMR §6.9 on hierarchy). Participants sign up through a QR form, and quotas fill first-come within each cell. Where possible, sample across departments and shifts.

6. Sample size reasoning

The total of n ≈ 3,000 matches what other countries used to build national panels: China 3,000, Malaysia 3,324, NIOSH 3,997. Each component below checks that the numbers are enough for the specific estimate.

  1. Percentiles per zone (O1/O3). Panel limits are set by the tails (about the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles). With SD ≈ 7 mm for face width, the standard error of the 5th percentile is √(p(1−p)/n) ÷ f(xₚ). With n = 500 per zone that is about 0.66 mm, so the 95% CI is roughly ±1.3 mm. NIOSH cells are 10–12 mm wide, so zone-level tails are estimated well inside a cell’s width.
  2. Coverage gap by zone × sex (O2). Expect about 15% outside the NIOSH grid; Malaysia found 10.8% and Taiwan about 30%. With n = 250 per zone × sex, the 95% CI is about ±4.4 percentage points. Nationally (n = 3,000) it is about ±1.3 pp.
  1. 3D scans for shape analysis (O4). NIOSH built its five headforms from about 1,000 scans, China from 350. Phone scans of all 3,000 participants, plus 600 reference scans, is ample.
  2. Fit testing (O5) on n = 1,200, 40% of participants and 200 per zone, chosen to keep every cell populated:
    • Detects a 10 pp difference in pass rate between women and men (70% vs 80%) with 80% power at α = 0.05. This needs about 290 per group; we have about 600.
    • Gives zone-level pass rates per model to about ±6.4 pp.
    • Supports a regression of log fit factor on about 10 dimensions with ample events: about 8,000 mask tests, of which perhaps 2,000–3,000 fail.
  3. Phone validation (O7) on n = 600 reference-scanned plus callipered participants, stratified across cells. This gives Bland–Altman limits of agreement with about ±0.25 SD precision per dimension, by sex.
  4. Allow 10% loss (incomplete scans, withdrawn consent). Recruit about 3,300 to end with about 3,000.

7. Measurements

7.1 Every participant (about 30 min)

  • Demographics: age, sex, self-described community and language (optional and free-text, recorded as context, not as an ancestry claim), state of birth, cadre, height, weight, neck circumference, facial hair, spectacles, dentures.
  • Calliper anthropometry: the ISO 16976-2 / NIOSH 10 PCA dimensions (minimum frontal breadth, face width, bigonial breadth, face length = menton–sellion, interpupillary distance, head breadth, nose protrusion, nose breadth, nasal root breadth, subnasale–sellion), plus the rest of the NIOSH 18–19 set, plus nasal-bridge breadth at two heights and the menton–chin arc. Spreading and sliding callipers with sliding-calliper nasal root attachments, following the published NIOSH 2003 protocol.
  • Phone 3D scan: a standardised older-generation iPhone with TrueDepth (one model across all sites), using a scanning app with mesh export (e.g. Heges) [TBC: app choice after pilot]. Use a neutral expression, hair cap, and stickers on 8 landmarks before scanning.
  • Seal-check questionnaire: prior respirator use, and any fit test ever done.

7.2 Validation subsample (20%, n = 600)

  • Reference scan with a handheld structured-light scanner (EinScan H2 or Artec Eva), one per hub.
  • A second measurer repeats 5 dimensions on 10% of participants to estimate inter-rater error.

7.3 Fit-test subsample (40%, n = 1,200; about 45–60 min)

  • Instrument: TSI PortaCount 8048 (N95 companion mode for N95s), OSHA modified ambient-aerosol CNC protocol (2019), with a fit factor of 100 or more as a pass for half masks.
  • Masks: 6–8 models in randomised order.
    • Disposable (5): two Indian-made N95s that are among the most procured (e.g. Venus, Magnum [TBC from KMSCL/AIIMS/HLL procurement data]), one cup-style (e.g. 3M 1860 or equivalent), one three-panel flat-fold, and one ear-loop “KN95/N95” of the type common in Indian markets.
    • Elastomeric (2–3): an Indian-made TPE half mask (e.g. Venus V-500/V-900 or Karam RS-1101) in every available size, plus one international reference EHMR.
  • Order per mask: don, user seal check (record what the participant judges), quantitative fit test, then 2 minutes of comfort and speech rating.
  • Leak location: for failed tests, record the leak site (nose / cheek / chin) by probe repositioning or a smoke-tube check. Run fluorescent-tracer leak mapping on a subset of 100 [TBC].
  • Beard protocol: bearded participants are tested with and without the beard cover.

7.4 Quality control

  • 5-day anthropometry training for all field staff, certified against a master measurer, with a target inter-rater technical error of measurement (TEM) of 1.5 mm or less on core dimensions.
  • Daily PortaCount checks; weekly re-measurement of 2 volunteers per site.
  • Central data review within 48 hours of each upload.

8. Analysis plan

  1. Descriptives. Weighted means, SDs and 1st/5th/50th/95th/99th percentiles for every dimension, overall and by sex, age band and zone. Publish the pooled distributions alongside the subgroup tables so the overlap between groups is visible.
  2. Comparisons.
    • Compare against NIOSH 2003, China 2008 and Malaysia 2022 means with standardised mean differences.
    • Use mixed models with a random site effect for zone, sex and age effects.
  3. Coverage of existing panels (O2). Plot every participant on the NIOSH/ISO bivariate grid (98.5–138.5 × 120.5–158.5 mm) and the PCA ellipse. Report the weighted share outside, and the share per cell compared with US shares.
  1. Indian panels (O3).
    • Bivariate: choose face length × width limits containing ≥95% of each sex, a 10-cell grid, and 25 subjects allocated in proportion to population share with at least 1 per cell (following Zhuang 2007 / ISO 16900-1).
    • PCA: run on the 10 dimensions, with an outer ellipse at 95%, 8 cells and 25 subjects.
    • Report sex-specific coverage.
  2. Shape analysis and headforms (O4).
    • Landmark-based Generalised Procrustes Analysis, then PCA on the scans, plus k-means or Gaussian-mixture clustering.
    • Choose the number of sizes k (2–6) that maximises coverage against the number of SKUs.
    • Build 3–5 mean headforms (for example S, M, L, long/narrow, short/wide) and test whether a “low nasal root” variant is needed.
  3. Fit models (O5).
    • Pass rate per model with 95% CI, by sex, zone and cell.
    • A mixed-effects logistic model of pass/fail on dimensions, model, sex, age, BMI and facial hair, with a random participant effect.
    • Compare traditional dimensions against 3D shape PCs for predicting fit (as Yu & Griffin 2026 did).
    • Portfolio analysis: find the smallest set of models and sizes that gets ≥90% (then 95%) of participants a pass.
  4. Seal check vs fit test (O6). Sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV.
  5. Phone validation (O7). Bland–Altman analysis per dimension; decide for each dimension whether phone measurements can be used, need correction, or must be dropped.
  6. Pre-registration. Pre-register the analysis plan (OSF and CTRI) before the main fieldwork.

9. Ethics, consent and data protection

  • Approvals:
    • Institutional ethics committee (IEC) at Ashoka plus each site, or a single lead IEC with site acceptance [TBC].
    • Prospective CTRI registration (not mandatory for an observational study, but cheap insurance).
    • If Phase 3 tests prototype masks on people, get CDSCO advice first, since PPE is a Class B device under MDR 2017.
  • Consent (ICMR 2017 §2.2, §5):
    • Written, in the local language, with an impartial witness for participants who cannot read.
    • Tiered consent for:
        1. use within the study;
        1. sharing of derived measurements;
        1. sharing of the raw 3D mesh under a data use agreement;
        1. access by a manufacturer partner under contract;
        1. any image in a publication (default no).
  • Hierarchy (§6.9): no recruitment through supervisors. Participation and results are never shared with employers. Participants can refuse or withdraw without penalty.
  • Tribal and Northeast communities (§6.7, §2.10): engage district administration and community or council leaders before fieldwork, set up a community advisory board in the NE and Adivasi zones, and handle Inner Line Permits for staff travel. Report results back to communities in plain language.
  • Benefit to participants: each participant gets their own fit result and a card listing which of the tested masks fit them. Sites get an aggregate report. Compensation is ₹500–1,000 for time and travel, set so it is not an undue inducement. Treatment for any research-related harm is free (§2.5–2.6).
  • DPDP Act 2023 / Rules 2025:
    • Ashoka is the Data Fiduciary.
    • DPDP-grade itemised notice and consent, with withdrawal as easy as giving consent.
    • Under-18s excluded.
    • Data processing agreements with any app, cloud or manufacturer partner.
    • Breach response within 72 hours.
    • Data stored in India, encrypted, with role-based access.
    • The research exemption (s.17(2)(b)) is not relied on, because each participant receives an individual fit result.
    • Core obligations apply from 13 May 2027, mid-study, so design to the full standard from day one.
  • Data release:
    • Open: derived measurements (de-identified), panel definitions, headform STL files, analysis code.
    • Controlled: face-cropped meshes under a data use agreement.
    • Never: textured face images.

12. Budget (indicative range, INR)

Assumptions: ₹88 per US$ [TBC at procurement]; about 30% added to US list prices for import duty, GST and shipping; salaries at typical Indian research-project rates; 24 months.

Cheaper variants: - Pilot alone (one site, n = 150, 6 months, borrowed or rented PortaCount): about ₹25–40 lakh. This is enough for a proof-of-concept paper, the first Indian pass rates and grant leverage. - Lean main study (n = 1,800 across 6 zones, PortaCounts rented or borrowed from NIOH or hospitals, fit-testing 600): about ₹2–2.5 crore. - Phase 3 co-design is extra. Most tooling costs should sit with the manufacturer.

Likely funders: - Coefficient Giving: its biosecurity RFP covers scalable respiratory protection and elastomeric stockpiles, with rolling submissions. - ICMR CAR (Medical Devices Mission): needs an eligible IIT as lead. - India Alliance Team Science. - BIRAC BIG, through a manufacturer or startup partner, for Phase 3. - Founders Pledge / Blueprint Biosecurity, for the policy work.

13. Expected outputs and impact

  1. The first estimate of how well the NIOSH/ISO panels cover Indian faces (paper 1, at about month 12).
  2. An Indian anthropometric database and fit panel (paper 2, plus an open dataset).
  3. Indian headforms (STL), usable by manufacturers and by BIS for IS 17274-5.
  4. Pass rates of the respirators India actually buys, and the smallest stockpile portfolio that covers ≥90–95% of health workers. This feeds straight into the BioPREVAIL stockpile argument.
  5. Evidence on seal checks vs fit tests, to support adding fit testing to MoHFW and state guidance.
  6. A BIS amendment proposal: select IS 9473 / IS 14746 TIL subjects from an Indian (or ISO-plus-Indian) panel.
  7. A manufacturer co-design pathway for sized elastomeric face seals.

14. Risks and mitigations

Risk Mitigation
Phone scans too inaccurate for lateral and depth dimensions Callipers on everyone for the core dimensions; phones used for shape only where validated
Northeast access (permits, trust) Local co-investigators, a community advisory board, early engagement, a longer lead time
Supervisor coercion in hospital recruitment Open invitation, private sign-up, no employer access to individual data
Industry conflict of interest Masks bought at market, not donated; manufacturers get no say in analysis; pre-registered analysis plan
PortaCount import delays Rent, or borrow from NIOH or hospitals; start the pilot with qualitative tests
Misuse of region or community findings for stereotyping Present results by measured size; show overlap; no “community mask” recommendations
DPDP compliance changing mid-study Build to full DPDP standards from day one
